# Channels — the constraint shapes the copy

Every channel below lists: the job, the hard constraints, a structure that works, and the
usual failure. Character limits move as platforms change — treat them as "design for this,
verify before a paid launch", not as constants.

## Email — subject and preheader

The subject sells the open, nothing else. Mobile clients cut around 35–45 characters, so the
decisive words go first. The preheader (roughly the next 35–90 characters shown in the list)
is a second headline, not a leftover — never let it default to "View in browser".

Works: specificity ("שלוש דקות ההכנה שנעלמו מהמשמרת"), an open loop, a name they recognize,
a number. Fails: cleverness with no content, ALL CAPS, "!!!", "Newsletter #14", and anything
that sounds like it was sent to ten thousand people.

## Email — body

Cold B2B: 50–125 words. One idea, one ask, no attachment, no logo block. Structure: one line
proving you know *their* business → the observation or offer → the smallest possible next
step. Warm or list email: 150–300 words, front-load the payoff, single CTA repeated at most
twice (top and bottom).

Failure: the "about us" paragraph. Nobody has ever bought because of it.

## WhatsApp

Effectively unlimited, which is exactly the trap. Three to five short lines. Their name, one
line of why-them, one line of value, one question answerable in a word. No greeting formulas,
no link in a cold first message — the reply earns the link. Voice notes only to contacts who
already voice-note back.

## SMS

Hebrew is encoded UCS-2, so a segment is **70 characters** (67 when concatenated) — not the
160 of Latin text. Plan for one segment. Identify the sender in the first three words, put the
single action at the end, include an opt-out where the list requires it.

## Landing / sales page

Above the fold, four things: what this is, who it is for, the main payoff, one CTA. If a
visitor cannot answer "what is this and is it for me" in five seconds, nothing below matters.

Section order that holds up: headline + subhead → the problem in their words → the mechanism
→ proof (named, numeric) → offer and terms → objections → CTA repeated. Long pages work when
each section stands alone, because people scan first and read second.

Failure: a hero image with a slogan and no sentence explaining what is sold.

## Meta ads (Facebook / Instagram)

Primary text truncates around 125 characters on mobile — the hook lives before the fold, and
"…See more" is earned, not assumed. Headline is roughly 27–40 characters. The first line must
work as a standalone thought. Match the ad's promise to the landing page's headline word for
word; mismatch is the most common cause of a high-CTR, zero-conversion campaign.

## Google search ads

Responsive search ads: headlines 30 characters, descriptions 90. Echo the query language in
at least one headline — relevance is both a conversion lever and a cost lever.

## Product page (Shopify)

First 2–3 lines carry the sale: what it is, what it replaces, the one reason to choose it.
Then scannable specifics — size, yield, storage, dilution, what it pairs with. Buyers of a
B2B consumable want operational answers (how many servings, how it stores, how fast to
prepare) far more than adjectives.

## Catalog / pricelist (print or PDF)

Print is read out of order, so every page must survive alone: product, what it does for the
business, price with the VAT basis stated, and the SKU. Keep one price grammar throughout the
document. Design constraints belong to the assets lane — copy's job is the line that makes a
buyer circle the item.

## Social captions

The first line is a headline; treat it as one. One idea per post. A CTA on a social post is
usually "tell me", not "buy now" — the platform rewards replies, and replies are what a sales
motion can actually use.

## Push / in-product notification

Under ~10 words, one specific noun, no cleverness. Interrupting someone's day requires being
useful within the first three words.
