# Frameworks — structure, leads, offers, objections

Contents: awareness · sophistication · frameworks · lead types · headline patterns ·
offer anatomy · objection order · proof ranking · CTA patterns · sequence maps.

Frameworks are scaffolding, not scripts. Pick one because it matches where the reader
stands, then let it disappear into the writing.

## Awareness (Eugene Schwartz) — decides the opening

| Stage | The reader | Opening move | Failure if you get it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem | Story, observation, or a number about their world | Product pitch reads as spam |
| Problem-aware | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | Dramatize the problem in their words, then reveal a category | Jumping to features feels random |
| Solution-aware | Knows the category, weighing options | Lead with mechanism — why this approach beats the others | Repeating the problem bores them |
| Product-aware | Knows you, hasn't bought | Proof, differentiator, risk reversal | Educating them wastes the open |
| Most aware | Ready, needs a reason to act now | Offer, terms, deadline | Any preamble loses the sale |

Most B2B outreach fails because it is written at "most aware" and sent to "unaware".

## Sophistication — how tired is the market of these claims?

| Level | Market state | What still works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First entrant | State the claim plainly. "Cold beverage base, ready to pour" |
| 2 | Competitors claim it | Enlarge the claim — bigger, faster, more specific |
| 3 | Everyone claims it | Lead with the **mechanism**: *how* you deliver it |
| 4 | Mechanisms are copied | Elaborate the mechanism, or make it demonstrably new |
| 5 | Cynical, all claims discounted | Identify with the reader's experience; sell identity and proof, not promise |

Israeli HoReCa suppliers are typically level 3–4: everyone says fresh, quality, service.
The mechanism and the proof are where the room is.

## Structural frameworks

- **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solve). Problem-aware readers. Agitation means consequences
  they already feel, not invented drama. Best for win-back and pain-led outreach.
- **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action). Cold, scrolling, low-commitment.
  Interest is earned with information; desire with the future-state picture.
- **BAB** (Before → After → Bridge). Fast, works in short channels. Before = today's mess,
  After = the specific better day, Bridge = you.
- **4 P's** (Picture → Promise → Prove → Push). Good for landing pages; proof is a section,
  not a sentence.
- **PASTOR** (Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response). Long-form sales
  pages where a customer story carries the proof.
- **QUEST** (Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition). B2B where the reader must
  self-select — opens by naming who this is *not* for, which raises credibility.
- **Star–Story–Solution.** The star is the reader or a customer like them, never you.

## Lead types (the first 100 words)

Offer · Promise · Problem–Solution · Big Secret (a mechanism they don't know) ·
Proclamation (a defensible claim about their world) · Story.
Cold and unaware audiences: story, problem, or secret. Warm and product-aware: offer or
promise.

## Headline patterns that carry weight

Each one below works because of the psychology in brackets — keep the psychology, change
the words.

- "How [specific person] got [specific result] without [the dreaded cost]" [proof + relief]
- "[Number] [things] that [outcome] — number [n] surprised us" [specificity + open loop]
- "The reason your [X] keeps [failing]" [diagnosis; you know something they don't]
- "If you [situation], read this before [deadline/decision]" [self-selection]
- "Stop [common practice]. Do this instead." [pattern interrupt + authority]
- "[Benefit] in [time] — here's the arithmetic" [promise anchored to proof]
- "What [respected group] knows about [topic] that you don't" [insider access]
- Question headline — only when the honest answer is "yes, that's me". A question they'd
  answer "no" to is a closed door.

Test a headline three ways: is it specific, is it *about them*, and could a competitor run
it verbatim? Failing the third means it says nothing.

## Offer anatomy

Promise · what they actually get · price and how it is framed (per unit? per cup? per month
against the cost of the status quo?) · risk reversal (the single biggest lever in B2B —
sample, trial, first order returnable) · the reason it exists now.
An offer that cannot be turned down is usually not cheaper — it is lower-risk.

## Objections — handle in this order

1. "I don't believe you." → proof, mechanism, specificity
2. "It won't work for me." → a case matching their exact situation
3. "I don't have time / it's a hassle." → show the smallest possible first step
4. "It's too expensive." → reframe against the cost of doing nothing, then risk reversal
5. "I'll do it later." → cost of delay, real deadline, or the natural trigger moment

Answer objections in the body *before* the CTA. An objection surfacing after the ask is a
lost reader.

## Proof, strongest to weakest

Named customer with a number → a demonstrable number of your own → third-party/lab/standard
→ a mechanism explained credibly → unnamed customer quote → volume claims ("thousands of…")
→ adjectives. Anything below the mechanism line is nearly weightless; anchor there or higher.

## CTA patterns

Low friction ("reply with one word", "send 'kit'") · specific and dated ("pick a slot this
week") · benefit-restated ("get the tasting kit") · single-step-first ("see the three-line
price list").
One CTA per message. Two CTAs halve both.

## Sequence maps

- **Cold B2B (4 touches):** relevant observation about *their* business → useful thing with
  no ask → short case from their segment → direct ask with an easy first step. Each touch
  stands alone; assume the earlier ones were never read.
- **Win-back (3):** notice their absence plainly and without guilt → what changed since →
  a low-risk reason to restart. Never open with "we miss you".
- **Post-first-order (3):** make the first use succeed → the second use case they haven't
  tried → the reorder made trivial.
- **Launch (4):** problem → mechanism → proof → offer with deadline.
